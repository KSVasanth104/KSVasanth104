# Show Bible: "God Is Near" (working title)

This file is the single source of truth for the show. Claude reads it at the start of
every session. Every script, storyboard and Midjourney prompt must follow it.

Alternative titles to choose from: *Bhagwan Saath Hai*, *Raghu's Guardians*, *Daily Darshan*.

---

## 1. Concept

An ordinary Indian boy, **Raghu**, lives an ordinary life, and that life is hard: money
trouble, exams, bullies, illness, loss, injustice, temptation. When a hardship reaches
its breaking point, a **Hindu deity physically steps into his world**, sometimes in true
divine form and sometimes disguised as an ordinary person, animal or object.

**The deity never simply fixes the problem.** They give Raghu courage, a clue, a push,
or a moment of protection. **Raghu still makes the final choice and does the work.**
That is the heart of every episode: *God walks beside you, but you must walk.*

### Growing up across seasons ("everything a man faces")
| Season | Raghu's age | Life stage | Hardship themes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | Childhood | Bullying, exams, fear, poverty at home, losing a pet, lying, jealousy |
| 2 | 16 | Teenager | Peer pressure, failure, first heartbreak, a parent's illness, career confusion, social media, anger |
| 3 | 25 | Young man | Unemployment, workplace injustice, debt, loneliness, moving to the city, temptation of shortcuts |
| 4 | 40 | Family man | Supporting parents and children, health scares, loss of a parent, betrayal, mid-life doubt |

Start with **Season 1**. The same deities stay with him as he grows, which builds emotional payoff.

## 2. Format

- **Primary:** YouTube Shorts / Instagram Reels, **vertical 9:16**, **45–60 seconds**, ~8–10 shots.
- **Later:** 16:9 compilations or 5–8 minute long-form episodes.
- **Language:** _TBD_ (Tamil / Hindi / English). Scripts are written in English first and then translated.
- **Narration:** short, with mostly character dialogue plus a one-line moral at the end.

### Episode structure (60-second Short)
| Time | Beat | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | **Hook** | Strongest image or line first (e.g. Raghu crying, a bully raising his fist) |
| 3–15s | **Daily life** | Show the normal world: home, school, street |
| 15–30s | **Hardship builds** | The problem gets worse; Raghu feels alone |
| 30–35s | **Breaking point** | A silent prayer, a tear, or a moment of giving up |
| 35–48s | **Divine intervention** | The deity appears physically, in true form or disguise |
| 48–55s | **Raghu acts** | He makes the brave or right choice himself |
| 55–60s | **Moral and tease** | One-line lesson + hint at the deity (e.g. a peacock feather left behind) |

## 3. Characters

### Raghu (main character)
- 10 years old (Season 1), thin, medium-brown skin, big expressive dark eyes, messy black hair,
  small gap in his front teeth when he smiles.
- Wears a faded blue school uniform shirt with khaki shorts on school days, and an old orange
  t-shirt at home. Carries a patched school bag.
- A small black thread (kaala dhaaga) on his right wrist, tied by his grandmother. **This is his
  constant visual signature.**
- Personality: kind, curious, easily scared, honest but sometimes tempted, never gives up for long.

### Amma (mother)
- Late 30s, simple cotton saree, hair in a bun, small bindi, tired but warm eyes. Works as a tailor
  from home.

### Appa (father)
- Early 40s, moustache, auto-rickshaw driver, half-sleeve checked shirt and lungi or trousers.
  Proud and hard-working, worried about money.

### Paati / Dadi (grandmother)
- 70s, white or light saree, silver hair, glasses, prayer beads. She tells Raghu stories of the
  gods at night, which links the family world to the divine world.

### Supporting cast
- **Kiran:** Raghu's best friend, chubby, funny, loyal.
- **Vikram:** the school bully, tall, rich-kid sneakers, has his own hidden pain (redeemed later).
- **Meena Teacher:** strict but fair class teacher.
- **Tommy:** a street dog with one floppy ear who follows Raghu everywhere.

### Setting
A small town in South/Central India: narrow lanes, a one-room house with a small pooja shelf,
a government school, a banyan tree, a temple at the end of the street, a market, monsoon rain.

## 4. The deities: reverence rules and iconography

### Reverence rules (non-negotiable)
1. Deities are **always shown with dignity**: no slapstick at their expense, no mocking, no
   distorted or comedic body designs. Gentle humour (e.g. Krishna's playfulness) is fine.
2. **Iconography must be accurate and consistent** (see the table below).
3. Divine forms glow softly; light, flowers, wind or bells signal their arrival.
4. Deities **guide, protect and inspire**. They never do Raghu's moral choice for him.
5. No deity is ever shown as defeated, foolish or inferior to another deity.
6. Respect real traditions: when unsure about a detail, choose the most widely recognised depiction.

### Deity cast: which deity handles which hardship
| Deity | Domain in the show | True-form iconography (must match) | Disguise ideas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ganesha** | Obstacles, new beginnings, exams | Elephant head, broken right tusk, four arms, modak sweet, red/yellow dhoti, mouse vahana **Mooshika** | A plump sweet-shop owner, a small mouse that leads the way |
| **Hanuman** | Fear, bullying, physical danger, strength | Monkey face, strong body, saffron/orange skin or garments, **gada** (mace), devotion to Rama | A strong porter at the market, a monkey on the temple wall |
| **Krishna** | Friendship, tricky choices, lying, wisdom | Blue skin, **peacock feather** in crown, **flute**, yellow silk dhoti (pitambara), playful smile | A cowherd boy who plays flute under the banyan tree |
| **Saraswati** | Studies, art, speech, confidence | White saree, **veena**, white swan or lotus, book and rosary | A kind librarian, an old music teacher |
| **Lakshmi** | Money trouble, honesty with money, the home's prosperity | Red saree, seated on a **pink lotus**, gold coins flowing from her hand, elephants | A gentle woman buying from Amma's tailoring stall |
| **Durga** | Injustice, protecting the weak, standing up to evil | Many arms (8–10) holding weapons, red saree, **lion/tiger** vahana, calm fierce face | A lady police officer, a lioness-like strong neighbour |
| **Shiva** | Grief, loss, anger, letting go | Blue throat, **crescent moon** and Ganga in matted hair, **trishul**, **damaru**, snake around neck, tiger skin | A calm sadhu by the river, an old man at the cremation ghat |
| **Murugan** *(optional, for Tamil audience)* | Courage of youth, discipline | Young warrior, **vel** (spear), **peacock** vahana, rooster flag | A young athletics coach |

**Disguise rule:** a disguised deity always leaves **one tell-tale sign**, which is the end-of-episode
reveal (a peacock feather, a modak, a trail of lotus petals, the sound of a damaru, a swan feather).

### Video (Midjourney animate)
For every shot, write a short **motion prompt**: one clear action + one camera move
(e.g. "Raghu wipes his tears and looks up, slow camera push-in"). Keep it simple, because one
action per clip gives the most stable results.

## 7. Production pipeline

1. **Claude:** script → shot list → Midjourney image and motion prompts → voice lines.
2. **You:** generate images and clips in Midjourney and save them as `episodes/epXX/clips/shotXX.mp4`.
3. **Claude:** generate voice lines through ElevenLabs into `episodes/epXX/voice/` (if the API is reachable).
4. **Claude:** create `subtitles.srt` and `edit_plan.md`, and optionally an ffmpeg rough cut.
5. **You:** final edit in CapCut (music, sound effects, divine glow transitions, captions).
6. **Claude:** title, description, hashtags and thumbnail prompt.

## 8. Writing rules for Claude

- Follow the episode structure in section 2 and keep Shorts to 45–60 seconds.
- Every Midjourney prompt = shot description + character description + **master style block**.
- Every episode ends with a **one-line moral** and a **deity sign**.
- The hardship must feel real and relatable. Never resolve it with pure magic.
- Keep the deity reverence rules in section 4 in every script.
- Track the voice character budget for the free ElevenLabs plan.
