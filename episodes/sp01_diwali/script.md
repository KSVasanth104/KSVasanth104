# Diwali Special: "The Demon's Last Wish"

**Format:** YouTube Short / Reel, 9:16, ~66s rough cut (target ≤60s, see the edit plan for how to trim)
**Language:** English (translate to Tamil/Hindi later)
**Source story:** Narakasura Vadham, the Krishna-era legend behind **Naraka Chaturdashi** (Choti Diwali),
from the Bhagavata Purana and popular South Indian retellings.

---

## Why this story, and the hook

Most Diwali Shorts retell Rama returning to Ayodhya. This one has three emotional pulls that keep people watching:

1. **Curiosity:** *"Why do we light lamps on Diwali?"* The answer is a surprise: a demon asked for it.
2. **Emotional twist:** the demon could only be killed by **his own mother**, and she was reborn as Satyabhama.
3. **Redemption:** in his last breath he recognises her and asks the world to celebrate with light, not tears.

**Hook (0–4s):** open on the dying demon king holding a tiny diya with a tear on his cheek, and his line
*"Mother… let the world… light lamps for me."* The title card *"WHY DO WE LIGHT LAMPS ON DIWALI?"* sits on top.
The viewer has to stay to find out who his mother is and why a demon wants lamps.

**Frame:** Paati tells the story to Raghu on Diwali night, which keeps it inside the show's world.
It ends with Raghu lighting his own diya, and a **peacock feather** (Krishna's sign) is lying beside it.

### Reverence notes (bible §4)
- Some retellings have Krishna swoon from Narakasura's arrow. To follow rule 5 (no deity shown defeated), Krishna
  **chooses** to step back with a calm, knowing smile and gives the fight to Satyabhama. This is his leela,
  and it keeps the core of the legend: only the mother's hand can end it.
- Satyabhama's arrow ends him with **light, not blood**. Narakasura is a demon, but his final moment is
  played with compassion, not mockery.
- Krishna's iconography: blue skin, peacock feather in a golden crown, yellow pitambara, garland and divine glow.
